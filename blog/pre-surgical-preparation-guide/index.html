Pre-Surgical Preparation: How to Set Yourself Up for the Best Possible Recovery

March 12, 2026 — Wellness

The weeks before surgery present a valuable opportunity to optimize your body and environment for recovery. Research consistently shows that patients who prepare systematically for surgery experience less post-operative pain, recover faster, have fewer complications, and report higher satisfaction with their overall surgical experience. This guide outlines the evidence-based preparation strategies that make the biggest difference.

Physical Conditioning: Prehabilitation

Prehabilitation, the concept of improving physical fitness before surgery, has strong evidence supporting its effectiveness. Patients who enter surgery in better physical condition recover faster and with fewer complications. For orthopedic surgeries, prehabilitation typically focuses on strengthening the muscles around the joint that will be operated on, improving cardiovascular fitness through regular aerobic exercise, and optimizing range of motion.

Even modest improvements in physical conditioning make a meaningful difference. Studies of total knee replacement patients have shown that six weeks of pre-operative exercise, performed three times per week, resulted in significantly better post-operative function and faster achievement of recovery milestones compared to patients who did not participate in prehabilitation. The exercises need not be intense; walking programs, gentle strengthening exercises, and stretching routines all contribute to improved pre-surgical fitness.

Nutritional Optimization

Nutrition plays a critical role in surgical outcomes and recovery. Adequate protein intake is essential for wound healing and tissue repair. Patients should aim for a protein-rich diet in the weeks leading up to surgery, including lean meats, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, and nuts. Hydration is equally important, as well-hydrated tissues heal more efficiently. Vitamins and minerals that support healing, including vitamin C, zinc, and vitamin D, should be obtained through a balanced diet, with supplementation considered for patients with documented deficiencies.

Conversely, certain substances should be avoided before surgery. Alcohol consumption should be minimized in the weeks before surgery, as alcohol impairs immune function and wound healing. Tobacco use is one of the strongest risk factors for surgical complications and should be discontinued as far in advance of surgery as possible. Your surgeon may also recommend discontinuing certain supplements and medications that affect bleeding.

Mental and Emotional Preparation

The psychological dimensions of surgical preparation are often underestimated. Surgical anxiety is normal and expected, but excessive anxiety has been shown to worsen post-operative pain and delay recovery. Strategies for managing pre-surgical anxiety include learning about your procedure and recovery plan in detail, practicing relaxation techniques such as deep breathing and progressive muscle relaxation, establishing a support network of family and friends who will assist during recovery, and addressing specific fears or concerns with your surgical team.

Setting realistic expectations is particularly important. Understanding that some pain and limitation are normal after surgery, that recovery follows a gradual trajectory, and that good days and bad days are both part of the process helps patients maintain perspective and avoid discouragement during the early recovery period.

Preparing Your Recovery Environment

Before surgery, prepare your home to support a smooth recovery. Set up a comfortable recovery area with everything you will need within reach, including medications, water, phone, remote controls, and reading materials. If you will be using recovery devices such as a cold therapy unit or compression device, learn how to set them up and use them before surgery when you are feeling well and alert rather than trying to learn while medicated and in pain after the procedure.

Prepare meals in advance and freeze them, or arrange for meal delivery during the first week of recovery. Arrange transportation to follow-up appointments. If you have pets, arrange for someone to assist with their care during the period when your mobility will be most limited. These logistical preparations may seem mundane, but they significantly reduce stress and physical demand during the critical early recovery period.

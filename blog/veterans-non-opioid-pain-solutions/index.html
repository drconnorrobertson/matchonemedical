Veterans and Non-Opioid Pain Solutions: Breaking the Cycle

May 10, 2026 — Opioid Reduction

America's veterans have been disproportionately affected by the opioid crisis. Military service frequently involves physical trauma, chronic pain conditions, and the psychological toll of combat, creating a population with significant pain management needs. Historically, the Veterans Administration and military healthcare systems relied heavily on opioid prescriptions to address these needs, contributing to rates of opioid dependency among veterans that significantly exceed those of the general population. Breaking this cycle requires access to effective alternatives.

The Veteran Opioid Crisis

Veterans are prescribed opioids at roughly twice the rate of the general population, and veterans who served in combat zones are at even higher risk. The reasons are straightforward: musculoskeletal injuries from military service, chronic pain from repetitive physical stress, traumatic brain injuries, and the complex intersection of physical pain with post-traumatic stress and other service-related psychological conditions all drive high pain management utilization.

The consequences have been devastating. Veterans account for a disproportionate share of opioid overdose deaths. Opioid use disorder among veterans is strongly correlated with depression, social isolation, and suicide risk. The VA has acknowledged this crisis and has invested significantly in developing non-opioid pain management programs, but access to these programs remains uneven across the VA system and among veterans receiving care outside the VA.

Drug-Free Technologies for Veteran Pain Conditions

Many of the chronic pain conditions prevalent among veterans respond well to non-pharmacological treatment. Chronic back pain, the most common pain complaint among veterans, can be managed with a combination of thermal therapy for acute flare-ups, bracing for structural support, and progressive rehabilitation to strengthen the core and improve functional capacity. These approaches address the mechanical and inflammatory sources of pain rather than simply masking them with medication.

Joint pain from military service, including knee, shoulder, and ankle conditions related to the physical demands of military training and operations, responds well to thermal compression therapy. For veterans who have undergone surgical interventions for these conditions, multi-modality recovery protocols using cold compression, intermittent pneumatic compression, and microcurrent therapy provide effective post-surgical pain management without narcotics.

Traumatic Brain Injury and Microcurrent

Microcurrent therapy has shown particular promise for veterans dealing with the long-term effects of traumatic brain injury. Research indicates that sub-sensory electrical stimulation can promote neurological recovery, reduce neuroinflammation, and improve symptoms such as headaches, cognitive difficulties, and sleep disturbances that commonly follow TBI. While research is ongoing, early results are encouraging, and microcurrent therapy represents a drug-free option for a condition that has traditionally been managed primarily with pharmaceutical interventions.

Access and Coverage

Veterans have multiple pathways to accessing drug-free pain management devices. The VA healthcare system covers FDA-cleared medical devices when prescribed by a treating physician. Veterans with service-connected conditions may have particularly straightforward access to these technologies. For veterans receiving care through private insurance or TRICARE, standard insurance coverage and DME billing pathways apply.

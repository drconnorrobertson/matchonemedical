Navigating Insurance for Medical Devices: A Patient's Guide

April 04, 2026 — Wellness

Navigating insurance coverage for medical devices can feel overwhelming, but understanding the process and knowing what to expect makes it manageable. Whether you have been prescribed a thermal compression device, a back brace, a microcurrent therapy unit, or any other medical device, this guide walks you through the insurance process step by step.

Understanding Your Benefits

Medical devices are typically covered under the Durable Medical Equipment (DME) benefit of your insurance plan. This benefit is separate from your physician visit benefit and may have its own deductible, copayment, or coinsurance requirements. Before receiving any medical device, understanding your DME benefit helps you anticipate any out-of-pocket costs.

Key questions to ask your insurance company include whether your plan has a separate DME deductible and, if so, how much of it you have already met, what your coinsurance or copayment percentage is for DME items, whether prior authorization is required for the specific device you have been prescribed, whether your plan requires you to use a specific DME supplier or network, and whether there is an annual or lifetime maximum for DME benefits. Your insurance company's member services line can answer these questions, and the information is also typically available in your plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage document.

The Authorization Process

Many insurance plans require prior authorization before a medical device can be provided. This means that your physician submits documentation supporting the medical necessity of the device, and the insurance company reviews and approves it before the device is ordered. The authorization process typically takes three to ten business days, though urgent requests can sometimes be expedited.

The documentation required for authorization typically includes a prescription from your physician, clinical notes from a recent office visit that support the medical necessity of the device, relevant diagnostic information such as imaging results or surgical reports, and any plan-specific forms required by your insurance company. At Match One Medical, our insurance team handles the entire authorization process, submitting required documentation, responding to insurance company requests for additional information, and keeping you informed of the status.

Understanding Cost-Sharing

Even with insurance coverage, most patients have some out-of-pocket responsibility for medical devices. This typically takes one of three forms. A copayment is a fixed dollar amount you pay for each device or service. Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount that you are responsible for, typically 20 percent for most plans. A deductible is the amount you must pay before your insurance begins covering costs. Your specific cost-sharing depends on your plan's terms and where you are in your annual deductible.

For patients with high deductibles or significant coinsurance, the out-of-pocket cost of a medical device may be substantial. However, it is important to compare this cost against the alternative. The cost of a thermal compression device that prevents opioid dependency, for example, is minimal compared to the potential costs of addiction treatment. The cost of a properly fitted back brace is far less than the cost of complications from inadequate spinal support.

Financial Assistance Options

Patients who face financial barriers to accessing prescribed medical devices have several options. Manufacturer assistance programs may provide devices at reduced cost to qualifying patients. Payment plans can spread the cost over several months, making the expense more manageable. Flexible spending accounts (FSAs) and health savings accounts (HSAs) can be used to pay for medical devices with pre-tax dollars. Some charitable organizations provide assistance with medical device costs for patients who meet income criteria.

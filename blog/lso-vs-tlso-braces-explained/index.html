LSO vs. TLSO Back Braces: Understanding the Difference

January 28, 2026 — Back Bracing

Two of the most commonly prescribed back braces are the Lumbar-Sacral Orthosis (LSO) and the Thoraco-Lumbar-Sacral Orthosis (TLSO). While both serve the fundamental purpose of supporting and stabilizing the spine, they differ in the extent of coverage, the conditions they treat, and the degree of motion restriction they provide. Understanding these differences helps patients and prescribers make informed decisions about which brace is appropriate for a given clinical situation.

The Lumbar-Sacral Orthosis (LSO)

An LSO brace supports the lower back, spanning from the sacrum (the triangular bone at the base of the spine) to approximately the lower thoracic region, typically around the T10 to T12 vertebral levels. The primary function of an LSO is to limit motion in the lumbar spine while providing compression and support to the lower back musculature. LSO braces are available in rigid, semi-rigid, and flexible configurations depending on the degree of immobilization required.

Common indications for an LSO include lumbar disc herniation, lumbar degenerative disc disease, lumbar spinal stenosis, post-operative support after lumbar decompression or fusion surgery (when limited to the lower lumbar levels), spondylolisthesis of the lower lumbar spine, and acute or chronic lower back pain. The LSO is the more commonly prescribed of the two brace types because the majority of spinal conditions and surgical procedures involve the lower lumbar region.

The practical advantage of the LSO is its relatively modest profile. Because it does not extend up into the thoracic region, it allows normal upper body movement and is generally more comfortable for extended wear. Patients can sit, reach, and perform upper body activities with minimal restriction, which improves compliance and quality of life during the bracing period.

The Thoraco-Lumbar-Sacral Orthosis (TLSO)

A TLSO brace extends from the sacrum up to the upper thoracic region, typically to around the T3 to T6 vertebral level. This extended coverage provides immobilization and support across a much larger segment of the spine, encompassing the entire lumbar and thoracic regions. TLSO braces are almost always rigid or semi-rigid, as the clinical conditions requiring this level of coverage typically demand significant motion restriction.

TLSO braces are indicated for thoracic or thoraco-lumbar vertebral fractures, post-operative support after thoracic or thoraco-lumbar fusion surgery, scoliosis management in adolescents, thoracic kyphosis, and spinal conditions that involve the upper lumbar or thoracic segments. The extended coverage of the TLSO ensures that the involved spinal segments are adequately immobilized and supported throughout the healing process.

Key Differences in Practice

The most noticeable difference for patients is the extent of restriction. A TLSO significantly limits trunk rotation and forward bending across the entire torso, while an LSO primarily restricts lower back motion. TLSO braces typically extend around the chest and may include anterior chest panels or shoulder straps, making them more conspicuous under clothing and more restrictive during daily activities.

Comfort and compliance considerations differ between the two types. TLSO braces, due to their greater coverage and restriction, are generally less comfortable for extended wear. Patients may experience increased perspiration, skin irritation over a larger area, and greater psychological burden from the visible restriction. However, when a TLSO is clinically indicated, the importance of adequate immobilization outweighs these comfort considerations.

The Prescription Decision

The choice between an LSO and TLSO is a clinical decision that depends on the specific diagnosis, the vertebral levels involved, the surgical procedure performed (if applicable), and the patient's individual anatomy and risk factors. In general, the principle is to prescribe the least restrictive brace that provides adequate support and immobilization for the condition being treated. An LSO is preferred when the pathology is limited to the lower lumbar spine, while a TLSO is necessary when thoracic or upper lumbar segments require support.

Regardless of which brace type is prescribed, proper fitting is essential.
